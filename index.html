Automation Program Management InternPhoenix, Arizona, May to August 2026

I interned at TSMC's Arizona fab on the team that runs automated procedures. I proposed an audit of the runs that never started, scoped it, and led it across 130 procedures and more than 2,200 tools.

More on what I did

The platform reports how many of the runs it started were successful, and it had no record of the ones that never started. The audit found silent failures equal to 4 to 6 percent of the runs that did start.

The procedures had been written from inconsistent templates, so I turned them into detection specifications and found owners for them across several departments and with teams in Taiwan, where I worked in Mandarin. I wrote SQL and Python against more than 1,000,000 daily log entries and set up on-call alerts for critical misses.

I quantified about 600 hours a month of manual work and an estimated six figures in scrap, with wafers worth about $25,000 each, by prioritizing the fixes that protect wafer flow. I also built a reusable audit template organized by candidate type.

I shipped a key performance indicator across the whole platform, time saved per run, that measures what each automation is actually worth, along with four Power BI dashboards that are now in active use. The indicator was approved after I presented it to the head of manufacturing for Fab 21, and it has been implemented.

Presenting

I gave a research pitch as a Germination Project fellow on mental illness treatment and healthcare disparities among veterans and people experiencing homelessness. Our team had spent most of the project in the data. For the final presentation I took the ending and added a few short recollections from our interviews, along with my own personal connection to the topic. I kept them controlled and let them sit next to the numbers. The audience included Penn Medicine staff and local government leaders, and we won the pitch. The numbers made the case and the stories made it matter to the room, which is how I try to approach any presentation: who is listening, what they need to hear, and how to make it land for them.
